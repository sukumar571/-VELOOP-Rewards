Executive Summary
Get Paid To (GPT) platforms reward users for completing eligible digital activities such as surveys, app and game offers,
shopping-related actions, and small online tasks. These platforms connect users seeking rewards with advertisers seeking
measurable actions or consumer insights. Offerwall and research partners distribute campaigns and track qualifying completions;
the GPT operator manages the user experience, reward ledger, fraud controls, customer support and payouts.
This report reviews representative services—Freecash, Swagbucks, TimeBucks, JumpTask, EarnApp, Pawns.app and
Idle-Empire—along with earning methods, gamification, partner networks and monetization. It translates these market patterns into
recommendations for Veloop Rewards.
The main recommendation is to build trust before adding complex gamification. Users need clear task requirements, accurate
tracking, visible pending rewards, transparent withdrawal rules and a fair support process for missing credits. Once these basics
work, streaks, leaderboards, levels and a seasonal Level Pass can encourage repeat engagement.
Key findings and recommendations
  Reliability first: explain task conditions, reward status, eligibility, payout rules and support options.
  Test incentives: a $1 withdrawal minimum, a 7-day streak boost and a $100–$500 prize pool are hypotheses, not guaranteed
 best practices.
 Protect unit economics: account for user rewards, reversals, payment fees, support and fraud losses.
  Responsible gamification: disclose rules and odds for chance-based features; do not imply rewards are guaranteed.
  Measure outcomes: track activation, approved tasks, retention, payout success, disputes, fraud and net revenue.
 
Scope
This is a strategy report based on the assignment brief, not a live audit of every provider. Features, campaign availability and
payment methods can vary by country and change over time. Current terms must be verified with each provider before launch.

1. Platform Overview and Competitive Landscape
A GPT platform is a digital rewards service that lists eligible activities and credits rewards when a partner confirms completion. A
typical flow is registration → task discovery → eligibility check → task completion → partner validation → pending or approved
reward → withdrawal. Some validations take time; some actions may be rejected if campaign requirements are not met.

2. Earning Methods and User Journey
The reward offered depends on campaign terms, user location and eligibility, task difficulty and advertiser value. A displayed
reward should not be treated as guaranteed until the campaign's completion and validation rules have been satisfied.
Surveys
Market-research questionnaires collect opinions or demographic insights. Users may be screened out if they do not match the
target audience. Explain estimated time, reward and screening rules where available.
Offerwalls
Third-party catalogues may include registrations, trials, subscriptions and qualifying actions. Clearly disclose purchase
requirements, recurring billing, cancellation windows and exclusions.
App downloads and game milestones
Users install an app or game and reach a milestone within a time limit. Tracking may require a new install, permissions or
completion on the same device. State requirements before the task begins.
Playing games
Some offers reward qualifying playtime or achievements. Distinguish verified activity from time spent and avoid promising that all
gameplay automatically earns rewards.
Ads and videos
Where available, promotional views may provide small rewards. Availability can vary, so do not promise a permanent supply of
videos.
Micro-tasks
Tasks may include categorization, image labeling, testing or other digital work. Use clear instructions, quality checks and an
appeals process for rejected work.
Referrals
Define attribution, qualifying events, commission rates, caps, payout timing and anti-abuse rules. Rates vary by platform and
campaign.
Daily check-ins
Daily incentives can build habits. Explain what counts as a valid day, time-zone handling, reset rules and any streak exceptions.
Recommended task lifecycle
1. Show offer and eligibility. 2. User accepts terms. 3. Partner tracks completion. 4. Reward appears as pending. 5. Partner
validates or rejects it. 6. Approved balance becomes withdrawable. 7. User submits cash-out request and sees status updates.

3. Key Features, Gamification and UI/UX
Gamification uses goals, feedback and progress to make an experience motivating. It should reinforce genuine eligible activity, not
hide important terms or imply that uncertain rewards are guaranteed.
Daily check-ins and streaks
A streak calendar makes progress visible. Explain what counts as a valid day and when the streak resets.
Leaderboards
Show the scoring method, time window, tie-break rules, eligibility and prize distribution. Consider tiers for new users.
Spin wheels and mystery rewards
Chance-based bonuses need clear rules, limits, expiry terms and probability disclosures where applicable.
Badges and levels
Badges recognize milestones. Level benefits should be carefully costed and should not imply guaranteed earnings.
Wallet and reward ledger
Separate available, pending, rejected and withdrawn balances. Every entry should show date, source, status and explanation.
Withdrawal wizard
Display minimum threshold, fees, estimated processing time, supported methods, verification requirements and request history.
Recommended dashboard
  Top summary: available balance, pending rewards, lifetime rewards and withdrawal status.
  Main task area: featured offers, surveys, game milestones and category filters.
  Progress area: streak, level, badges and clearly explained leaderboard position.
  Trust area: recent activity, missing-credit support, payment FAQ and account security.
 
Registration and accessibility
Keep sign-up short and collect only necessary information. Offer email and supported social sign-in where feasible. Design for
mobile screens, readable text, keyboard navigation and clear error messages. Do not advertise a Google, Apple or Steam
integration unless it is implemented.

5. Monetization and Business Model
GPT platforms commonly earn revenue through performance marketing and market research. A partner pays when a defined
event is accepted, such as a qualified lead, completed survey, eligible install or in-game milestone. The platform shares an agreed
portion with the user and retains the remainder to fund development, operations and profit. This can be summarized as B2B
performance advertising plus user rewards and research participation.
The initial brief mentions a 50%–70% user payout share. Treat this as a planning assumption, not a verified universal benchmark.
Actual economics depend on campaign, country, contract, conversion validation, fraud, payment fees and support costs.

5. Monetization: Unit Economics, Controls and Risks
A sustainable model calculates net contribution from each valid activity, not just the gross amount reported by an offerwall. A
campaign that looks profitable before reversals or support costs can become unprofitable after rejected conversions, chargebacks
or fraud.
Illustrative unit-economics formula
Net contribution per approved action = partner revenue received − user reward − payment costs − expected reversal or fraud
losses − variable support and operating costs.
Example for planning only
If a partner pays $1.00 for an approved action and Veloop Rewards allocates $0.60 to the user, the initial gross difference is $0.40.
This is not net profit: fees, reversals, fraud prevention, support, taxes and operating expenses remain. The example is illustrative,
not a market quote.

6. Strategic Recommendations for Veloop Rewards
The following ideas are proposals to test, not commitments. Validate cost, user impact, abuse exposure and compliance
requirements before launch.
Recommendation 1 — Competitive daily leaderboard
A daily leaderboard can give users a short-term goal. The brief proposes a $100–$500 prize pool. Begin with a smaller capped
pilot or non-cash recognition, publish the scoring method and model maximum liability. Separate tiers can help prevent a small
group of high-volume users from dominating.
Recommendation 2 — 7-day streak multiplier
A 7-day streak could unlock a +5% boost on eligible rewards. Test whether retention and valid task completion justify the cost.
Define eligible tasks, expiry, reset rules, time zone and anti-abuse checks. Do not promise a permanent boost until the economics
support it.
Recommendation 3 — Low withdrawal minimum
A threshold such as US$1, or a suitable local-currency equivalent, may help users experience cash-out early. Very low thresholds
can raise processing costs and abuse. Compare payment fees, settlement delays, identity checks and partner rules, then publish
the threshold and processing estimate.
Standout idea — Gamified Level Pass
Introduce a seasonal Level Pass where eligible daily or weekly tasks unlock reward tiers. Possible rewards include bonus points,
limited-time perks, free spins or mystery boxes. A free-to-progress model is a sensible first test. If chance-based rewards are
included, disclose relevant odds and terms and review local rules.
Product principles
  Trust before excitement: make the wallet and payout rules dependable.
  Reward genuine value: prioritize validated actions over raw clicks.
  Keep incentives sustainable: cap experiments and review net contribution.
  Make terms visible: disclose eligibility, expiry, prize rules and limitations.
  Give users recourse: support missing or reversed rewards fairly.

7. Implementation Roadmap and Success Metrics
A phased rollout helps Veloop Rewards establish reliable operations before adding costly engagement features.
Phase 1 — Foundation
Build account security, task catalogue, offer details, reward ledger, pending/approved/rejected states, withdrawal requests, help
centre and audit logs. Test partner tracking end to end.
Phase 2 — Retention pilot
Add daily check-ins, streak tracking, simple badges and a limited leaderboard experiment. Cap the budget and publish eligibility
rules.
Phase 3 — Measurement and optimization
Compare cohorts, identify tracking drop-offs, inspect fraud and reversal rates, and adjust incentives based on net contribution
rather than gross revenue.
Phase 4 — Expansion
Evaluate additional offerwall partners and launch a seasonal Level Pass only when support, fraud review and payout operations
can handle the extra complexity.
Key performance indicators (KPIs)
  Activation: share of registered users who view and start an eligible task.
  Task conversion: share of started tasks that become partner-approved actions.
  Reward reliability: approval time, reversal rate and missing-credit tickets.
  Retention: day-1, day-7 and day-30 returning-user rates by acquisition source.
  Withdrawal health: successful payout rate, processing time, failure rate and support contacts.
  Economics: net contribution per approved action and incentive cost per retained user.
  Risk: suspected fraud, confirmed abuse loss, duplicate accounts and false-positive reviews.
 
Review cadence
Review product and support metrics weekly during pilots. Reconcile partner revenue, approved rewards, reversals and payouts at
least monthly. Pause or reduce any incentive whose cost, fraud or complaint levels exceed pre-agreed limits.

8. Conclusion, Glossary and Research Notes
GPT platforms sit at the intersection of performance advertising, market research and reward-based engagement. Their long-term
value depends on connecting legitimate user activity to verifiable partner outcomes while keeping rewards understandable,
payments dependable and costs controlled.
For Veloop Rewards, the recommended order is: first deliver a reliable task lifecycle and transparent wallet; next establish partner
tracking, fraud controls and support; then test streaks and leaderboards with capped budgets; finally introduce the seasonal Level
Pass if data supports it. A low withdrawal minimum and a large prize pool should be informed by local payment costs and
measured unit economics, not promised before validation.
